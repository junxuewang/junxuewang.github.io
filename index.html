Brain-Inspired Computing
and Neural Dynamics

I am a Ph.D. student at the School of Artificial Intelligence, Tianjin University. My research interests include brain-inspired computing, spiking neural networks, and temporal information processing.

My research explores how heterogeneous synaptic dynamics and adaptive temporal integration can improve neural computation. I am also interested in developing computational models that connect biologically inspired mechanisms with practical artificial intelligence systems.

I work on neural architectures and synaptic mechanisms for representing information across multiple timescales, with the broader goal of building more flexible and biologically inspired learning systems.

Research Interests

My research focuses on temporal computation and biologically inspired neural architectures.

01 / NEURAL COMPUTATION

Spiking Neural Networks

Exploring neural and synaptic dynamics for efficient temporal information processing in spiking neural networks.

02 / SYNAPTIC DYNAMICS

Heterogeneous Synapses

Investigating diverse synaptic timescales and their role in temporal integration and neural computation.

03 / TEMPORAL MODELS

Adaptive Temporal Integration

Developing adaptive synaptic filters and neural models for flexible temporal representation across tasks.
